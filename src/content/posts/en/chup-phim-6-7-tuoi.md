Six to seven is when everything starts moving at once: the front teeth begin to
change over, and the first permanent molar comes through at the back — that one
erupts on its own without replacing anything, which is why many parents never
notice it arrive.

This is the age at which **one panoramic radiograph becomes worth taking.**

## Four questions the eye cannot answer

A visual check only sees the part of a tooth above the gum. The rest — the roots,
the unerupted buds, the bone between them — is out of view. A panoramic
radiograph shows the whole jaw in one image, and answers:

**Are all the permanent tooth buds there.** Some children are missing one or
several from birth. Found early, a long-term plan is possible; found late, the
plan is reactive.

**Is any bud sitting the wrong way.** A tooth lying sideways in the bone will not
erupt on its own, and while it sits there it can press on the root of the tooth
next to it and dissolve it — quietly, painlessly, over years.

**Is there decay between the teeth.** This is the kind that sits where two teeth
touch. Nothing shows from the outside until the cavity is already large.

**Is there room on the arch.** Comparing the current arch width against the
combined width of the permanent teeth still to come. Crowding is something you
measure, not something you sense.

## Finding it early means treating it gently

This is the real reason to take the image at this age — not simply "to be sure".

Crowding found at seven can usually be managed with a removable appliance, which
the child takes out to eat and to brush. The same crowding found at fourteen,
once the jaw has all but stopped growing, may mean removing permanent teeth to
make room.

Same problem. Very different answers. The difference is when it was found.

## About the radiation

It is a fair question and it deserves a real answer rather than a brush-off.

At the clinic, images are taken in a separate shielded room, the child wears a
lead apron, and the dentist orders an image only when it will actually change the
treatment plan — not out of routine.

If you are still unsure, ask directly before you agree: *what question does this
image answer, and what do we lose by not taking it.* A clear answer is the best
sign you will get.
