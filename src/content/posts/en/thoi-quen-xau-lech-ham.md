An adult jaw has hardened and settled. A child's jaw is different: it is softer,
and more importantly **it is still growing** — which means it is still deciding
what shape it will end up.

A very small force, repeated daily over a few years, is enough to take part in
that decision.

## The mechanism: duration, not strength

This is the part that is easy to get wrong. People assume it takes a hard knock
to move a jaw. It is the opposite: **duration matters more than strength.**

A thumb resting in the mouth for a few hours every night, for three years, acts
on growing bone far more than a single fall does. Bone responds to sustained
force by remodelling itself around it.

## Habits worth watching

**Thumb and lip sucking.** The most common one, and the longest-lasting, because
it is tied to how the child self-soothes when sleepy or anxious.

**Mouth breathing.** Usually not a habit but a consequence — chronic nasal
congestion, allergic rhinitis, or enlarged adenoids. When the mouth is always
open, the lips and tongue no longer sit at their normal rest position, and the
balance of forces around the arch shifts.

**Tongue thrusting on swallowing.** A person swallows hundreds of times a day. If
the tongue presses against the back of the front teeth each time, that is
hundreds of small forces daily.

**Chewing on one side only.** Usually starts with a painful tooth on the other
side. The child avoids that side, and keeps avoiding it long after the tooth has
been treated.

**Nail biting, pen chewing, biting hard objects.** These act on a few teeth
rather than the whole arch.

## What to do

Three things, in order:

1. **Notice, do not scold.** Most of these happen outside the child's awareness,
   especially during sleep. Punishment usually teaches concealment, not change.
2. **Look for the cause underneath.** Mouth breathing almost always has a nasal
   or throat cause — deal with that and the habit tends to ease on its own.
3. **Have it looked at early.** The question is not "does my child suck their
   thumb" but "has the habit already changed anything on the arch". That is
   measurable at an examination, and the earlier it is measured, the more of the
   gentle options remain.

Not every habit leaves a mark, and not every child with a habit needs treatment.
But if a habit is still going after the teeth begin to change over, that is the
point to have someone look once.
