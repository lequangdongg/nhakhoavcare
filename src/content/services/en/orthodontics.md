Orthodontics moves teeth into position using light continuous force, either
through fixed braces or clear aligners.

## How long it takes

Most cases run from one to three years, depending on how much movement is needed
and the patient's age. Adult bone remodels more slowly, so adult treatment usually
takes longer.

## Retainers

Once the braces come off, teeth tend to drift back. A retainer is required, not
optional. Stopping retainer wear is the most common reason orthodontic results
are lost a few years later.

## Braces or aligners

Aligners look better but depend entirely on the patient wearing them enough hours
each day.

Invisalign aligners are manufactured from a digital model taken with the
[iTero scanner](/en/technology/) — no impression material, and you see the
predicted result before treatment begins.

## Early orthodontics for children

In young children the job is usually not to move teeth but to guide the jaw while
it is still growing. That is a separate branch, done with a removable appliance,
and it is only possible within a limited window.

See [children's dentistry](/en/childrens-dentistry/) for when to bring a child in.
