A gummy smile is when a lot of gum shows when smiling. It is not a condition in
itself but a sign of several quite different causes — and the right treatment
depends entirely on identifying which one.

There are three groups, and they are handled in completely different ways.

## Gum covering too much of the tooth

The teeth are actually long enough but partly hidden. This is the mildest group:
gum contouring exposes the correct proportion of the tooth.

## An upper lip that travels too high

The upper lip muscle is more active than usual, lifting the lip high on smiling
and revealing the gum above. The teeth and the bone are both normal — the issue
is muscle force.

This is treated at the lip, not the gum. Contouring here achieves nothing.

## Vertical overgrowth of the upper jaw

The jaw itself is longer than usual, so gum shows even when both the gum and the
lip are normal. This is the most involved group and the only one that needs
skeletal correction.

## Why the diagnosis comes first

All three causes look very similar from the outside. A photograph of a smile
cannot tell them apart.

They are separated by clinical examination — measuring tooth proportions and how
far the upper lip travels on smiling — combined with [imaging](/en/technology/)
to show the jaw relationship.

This is why the examination matters so much here: treat the wrong cause and the
gum still shows, and the money is still gone.
